# Layout notes: Magic Kingdom Tomorrowland (2026)

These notes record how the 3D model's layout was set, what it is based on, and which points still need Bryan to confirm.

**Research limits:** in this session web search worked but page fetches did not (DNS blocked). So the findings below come from search-result summaries of the sources listed, plus general knowledge of the park. None of it is from a direct read of an official map. Points marked **[confirm]** are my best placement and should be checked against the current park map or Bryan's memory.

## Orientation used in the model

- Units are about 2 m. x runs east and z runs south, so north is up on the top-down view (`?view=top`).
- **Castle hub** is to the **west**. Tomorrowland is the east land of Magic Kingdom, reached by a bridge over the hub moat.
- **Fantasyland** (and Storybook Circus) is to the **north / north-west**.
- **Main Street, U.S.A.** is to the **south-west**.
- **Space Mountain** sits at the **north-east edge**, outside the Walt Disney World Railroad berm.

## Space Mountain design references

The refurbished Space Mountain blends two real buildings. Only written descriptions were used for proportions and details. No official art was copied.

- **Disneyland Paris Space Mountain.** It is a 16-sided building, about 62 m across and 43 m high, with a Victorian, plate-and-rivet exterior. A Columbiad cannon is angled at about 32 degrees, with gold-leaf decoration. Sources give it as 22 m long or as 105 ft for the whole stand **[sources disagree]**. The model borrows the riveted bronze, the brass hoops and a cannon at about 32 degrees on a cradle.
- **Tokyo Disneyland Space Mountain, opening 2027.** Construction reports describe curved white panels arranged in a row that swoop across the building. They also note thin white strips and a circle of white spires at the top. The model borrows the sleek white shell, the sweeping fins, a swooping ribbon and the crown of spires.
- The Meridian Starport backstory on the card is original.
