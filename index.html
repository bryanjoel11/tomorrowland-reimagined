    Each one starts from something I saw on shift. Every card says what changed in the land, why it is worth doing, and what it adds for guests and for the operation. Items tagged [verify] are working assumptions I still need to confirm.

      About the project

      Why I rebuilt Tomorrowland

      I worked attractions in Tomorrowland at Magic Kingdom. Every shift I saw the same things: a Speedway queue that barely moved, guests in wheelchairs who could not ride the PeopleMover with their family, a Space Mountain that looked tired next to TRON, and a dark show building on the busiest stretch of the promenade.

      This project is the land the way I would run it. It is a 3D model laid out to match the real Tomorrowland, from the hub bridge and Rocket Tower Plaza to Space Mountain and TRON, with four changes I would make from the operator's side, shown in place.

      What it shows

      Operations insight turned into a proposal. I would swap the Speedway for Sugar Rush, an indoor dual racing coaster, and give the PeopleMover ramp-equipped cars and a stop-to-board zone. I would refresh Space Mountain as Hyperspace Mountain and reopen Stitch's Great Escape as an all-ages show. Each card says what changed and why.

      The Meridian Starport

      I wanted Space Mountain to have a story of its own, so I wrote one. A Victorian launch cannon that never fired, a white starport grown around it a century later, and a light-speed jump that finally sends it off. The building blends old-world brass and rivets with a sleek white sculptural shell.

      How it is built

      Vite, TypeScript and three.js. Every building, ride and path is generated in code: no downloaded models, logos or official artwork. Instancing keeps the track, rivets and trees fast, and the site is fully static.

      What is next

      Next I am confirming the [verify] items with people who still work the land and tightening the layout details I flagged in the project notes.
